# Personal Brand Positioning

> **Version:** 1.0
> **Last updated:** November 2026
> **Scope:** Professional positioning for content, marketing and AI-assisted work

## Purpose

Help a professional define a clear positioning statement and the content pillars that support it. The output is a short positioning document that can guide a portfolio, a LinkedIn profile and a content calendar.

The prompt does not invent achievements. Every claim about experience must come from the person's own material.

## Use case

Use this prompt when:
- a portfolio or profile says too much and the reader cannot tell what the person does,
- you are starting a new content series and need clear topics,
- you want to check whether your public work matches the role you want.

## Input requirements

- a list of past roles and projects, with real outcomes where available,
- the audience you want to reach,
- three to five topics you can talk about with confidence,
- topics you want to stop being associated with,
- the portfolio or profile text you currently use.

## Prompt

```text
You are a brand strategist who helps professionals define a clear, honest positioning.

PROFESSIONAL HISTORY AND PROJECTS
[Paste the list. Include outcomes only where they are real.]

TARGET AUDIENCE
[Who you want to be hired by, followed by or hired as a freelancer.]

TOPICS I CAN SPEAK ABOUT CONFIDENTLY
[List three to five.]

TOPICS I WANT TO STOP BEING ASSOCIATED WITH
[List them, or write "none".]

CURRENT PORTFOLIO OR PROFILE TEXT
[Paste it.]

TASK
1. Positioning statement: one sentence in the form "I help [audience] [achieve outcome] by [method]". Offer three options.
2. Evidence check: for each option, list which items in the history support it. Mark any claim with no evidence as [NO EVIDENCE].
3. Content pillars: three pillars, each with a one-line description and two example post ideas. Pillars must connect to the positioning statement.
4. Gaps in the current portfolio: where the existing text is generic, unclear or does not support the positioning. Name the section and the problem, not just "improve the copy".
5. First three changes: the smallest changes that would make the portfolio match the positioning.

RULES
- Use only the history provided. Do not add roles, employers, certificates, numbers or client names.
- Avoid buzzwords such as "thought leader", "guru" or "passionate".
- Keep the statements specific enough that a reader could disagree with them.
```

## Expected output

- three positioning statements with evidence checks,
- three content pillars with example posts,
- a list of portfolio gaps,
- three first changes.

## Quality criteria

1. Each positioning statement is supported by at least one real item from the history.
2. Pillars are distinct and connect to the statement.
3. Gaps name specific sections and problems.
4. No claim appears that the history does not support.

## Human review

- Confirm each claim against your own records.
- Choose the statement that feels true when you say it aloud, not the most impressive one.
- Review the pillars against your last ten posts. If they do not appear there, decide whether you want to start.

## Example (illustration)

Positioning option: "I help content teams publish AI-assisted material that has been checked against its sources, by designing review workflows."
Evidence: the case studies on content review and fact-checking in this library.
Pillar: "Editorial verification" with example posts on checking quotes, dates and numbers in AI drafts.
